Questions About the Work

Is a maintenance contract already paying for work on these ducts?

A tenant may use the ducts without owning them, and a building contract may set who can order work. If the invoice goes to the wrong party, or covers work a maintenance contract already pays for, payment for completed work could be disputed or delayed.

  • Identify the system and who controls it

    Say whether the space is served by an air handler, a rooftop unit or ducts shared with other tenants, and photograph any labels and ceiling access you can see. Put these questions to the provider: which ducts the proposal covers, what working hours and protection for ceiling tiles it assumes, and whether the owner, a property manager or a maintenance contract holder must approve before the first payment is made for your Woodbridge premises.

  • Tenant approval against landlord approval

    Compare approval by the tenant with approval by the landlord or a condominium corporation. Ask which one the landlord or property manager says applies, whether a maintenance contract already covers any of these ducts, and whose name should sit on the proposal and the invoice.

  • Invoice that names each task

    The written scope should list each area or duct run finished and the date and person who accepted each task; if the kitchen exhaust is part of the work, it should be named on its own, since it is a separate system and has its own requirements. The invoice should name the party who authorized the work and match each payment to a recorded task, with anything added later listed on its own line.
